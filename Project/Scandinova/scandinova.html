Social Change and Social Democracy (1920-1929)

Denmark

  • In 1871, Denmark's Social Democratic Party was founded, and it became the ruling party in 1924.
  • The party failed to resolve the deflation but their methods exacerbating the decline of industrial and agriculture section , contributing to its loss of power in 1926(Glückstadt et al., n.d.).

Norway

  • Norway's labor party was founded in 1887.
  • In 1928, the party won the election and formed the first labor government in Norway, but it only laster for 2 weeks.
  • In 1930s, the party gave up the line of revolution and started to follow rge reformist line. (Labour Party (Norway), n.d.)

    • Sweden

    • Sweden's Social Democratic Party was found in 1889. It initially followed the Marxist ideology (Bengtsson, 2019).
    • The party splitted in 1917. Some members left and eventually formed the Communist Party, and the rest decided to pursure a reformist path

    Great Depression (1929)

    Three countries abandoned the gold standard to devalue their currency

    Sweden

    • In September 1931, Sweden abandoned the gold standard after pressure on its currency. The Swedish krona depreciated, allowing monetary policy to focus on stabilising domestic prices rather than maintaining convertibility into gold (Türegün,2017).
    • Compared with the United States, Sweden experienced a less severe decline in industrial production. Its early departure from the gold standard and price-stabilisation policies helped limit the downturn (Jonung, 1981).

    Denmark

    • In 1933, the Kanslergade Agreement introduced measures including currency devaluation and the restriction of strike rights.(Stauning et al., n.d.)
    • However, the agreement is primarily aimed at prohibit the lockout that employers call for wage cut
    • The agreement established the substitute system, considered as the Early Stages of the Welfare State (Linton,n.d)

    Norway

    • Before the Great Depression, Norway had already been experiencing economic difficulties for several years, and the Great Depression exacerbated the situation.
    • From 1930 to 1931, industrial production of Norway fell by 22 percent, the unemployment rate among union members reached 33.4 percent(Tvedt,n.d)
    • The economy recovered slightly.industrial production increased by 75 percent during the years 1913-1938. However, the unemployment rate remain persistently high until World War II

    World War II

    • In 1939, Sweden declared itself neutral. However, Sweden allow German troops to cross their land to avoid military conflicts.

    • Norway declared neutrality in 1939, but was invaded by Germany soon. Because the French front was becoming critical, the Allies in Norway diverted to France. Norway was rapidly occupied

    • Denmark was occupied by Germany, while Iceland, which belonged to Denmark at that time, are under the control of the British military. Combined with the fact that they had wanted to separate from Denmark for a long time, Iceland became independent republic through a referendm
