Economy Crisis and Social Change(1920-1940)

Social Democracy Gained influence

  • The Industrial Revolution expanded the labor group, intensifying the conflicts between the classes
  • During the 1920s, communists gained influence, but lost power soon because of failures in solving economic problems (Glückstadt et al., n.d).
  • Social democracy abandoned the Marxist ideology about revolution and started to pursue a reformist path, and regained influence in regional politics. (Bengtsson, 2019)
  • About why socialism did not become prevalent in Scandinavia. One possible explanation is that the Nordic countries often conflict with the Soviet Union due to geopolitics, and they did not want to become too close to the USSR. (For instance, the Norwegian Labour Party split to quit the Communist International in 1923)

The Great Depression

  • During the 1930s, the great depression spread to Scandinavia, the unemployment rate increased, and industrial production decreased. For instance, Norway fell by 22 percent (Tvedt,n.d).
  • Three abandoned the gold standard to devalue their currency to boost exports. Sweden's economic condition improved, while Denmark's and Norway's economic conditions kept worsening. (Stauning et al., n.d.; Tvedt, n.d.)
  • The possible reason is that Denmark's economy relied on exports to the UK, while the UK adopted conservative policies to protect the democratic economy, which further worsened Denmark's economy.
  • In 1933, Denmark signed the Kanslergade agreement, which allowed the further devaluation of currency. the agreement also restricted the strike action from both the labor and the employer, to stabilize wages. (Stauning et al., n.d.)
  • Norway's economic crisis continued until World War II.

World War II and reconstruction

German Invasion

In 1939, Scandinavian countries declared neutral, but German occupy Norway and Denmark in 1940. To avoid military conflicts, Sweden repeatedly compromised to allow German troops to pass through its land. The government of Denmark was exiled to Britain.

Iceland, which had belonged to Denmark, was occupied by Britain. In 1944, Iceland became an independent republic through a public vote

Despite Norway and Denmark being occupied, the society still organized a small resistance movement. For instance, Denmark helped Jews hide in 1943

After war reconstruction

Norway focused on physical reconstruction after World War II. The country reused some of the infrastructure left behind by German troops to support rebuilding efforts. For instance, they converted a submarine base into shopping malls (Haugan,2023).

Sweden continued to establish its welfare state. introduced a universal social welfare system, including free healthcare, family support, and pensions. Due to the stable trading conditions, the economy recovered quickly (Sandvik, 2025).

Denmark was solving security problems. They recognized Iceland's independence, expelled the stationed Soviet troops, and joined NATO in 1949.
